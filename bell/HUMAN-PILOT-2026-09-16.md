# Bell human pilot

Purpose: test whether a crypto investor or analyst can use Bell without being
briefed by the builder. This is a usability and demand check, not an
investment-performance study.

## Participant profile

Recruit six people:

- four people who have used crypto markets or tokenised assets;
- two analysts, researchers or portfolio professionals who regularly compare
  instruments.

Do not show the product thesis, jury reports or competitive dossier before the
task. Tell participants only that Bell is a public research tool for tokenised
stocks, ETFs and commodities.

## Task script

Give each participant one randomly assigned case:

| Case | Prompt | Expected product state |
|---|---|---|
| Silver | “You are considering tokenised Silver. Decide whether you can compare the available wrappers.” | `COMPARISON WITHHELD` |
| Marvell | “You want to compare tokenised Marvell wrappers for a research shortlist.” | `FACTUAL COMPARISON OPEN` |
| SPY | “You are screening tokenised SPY before writing a memo.” | `COMPARISON WITHHELD` |
| AAL | “You found one tokenised American Airlines representation. Decide what Bell can and cannot compare.” | `SINGLE REPRESENTATION` |

Ask the participant to think aloud and complete the path without assistance:

1. Search the assigned reference.
2. Explain the state in their own words.
3. Open the evidence and identify the next action.
4. If Bell leaves a factual comparison open, select two wrappers and inspect
   the facts-only comparison. If Bell withholds comparison or returns a single
   representation, follow that route and record the next check instead.
5. Export the decision brief.

Stop the timer when the participant can answer the three questions below and
has downloaded the brief. Do not correct them during the task. Record every
help request and every unsupported investment conclusion.

## Questions after the task

Ask verbatim:

1. “What decision does Bell help you make?”
2. “What does this result allow you to do next?”
3. “What does Bell still not prove?”
4. “What would make you use this again for another asset?”
5. “What, if anything, was confusing?”

## Scorecard

| Measure | Record | Target |
|---|---|---|
| State comprehension | correct state explanation without help | at least 5/6 |
| Next-action comprehension | names the correct evidence or diligence step | at least 5/6 |
| Boundary comprehension | does not call a clean result approval | 6/6 |
| Task completion | reaches evidence and brief export | at least 5/6 |
| Time to decision | seconds from landing to state explanation | median under 120s |
| Help requests | count per participant | median 0 |
| Repeat-use signal | says what future asset they would run | at least 4/6 |

## Interpretation

Do not average away a safety misunderstanding. If any participant calls
`FACTUAL COMPARISON OPEN` a buy approval, treat that as a release blocker and
fix the wording before claiming success.

The pilot supports a near-100 usability claim only if the targets above are
met and the participant can name one unresolved input Bell does not provide.
It does not prove willingness to pay. A second-use interview or a paid pilot
is required for commercial demand.
