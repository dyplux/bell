Check it in 30 seconds, no key required

Clone the repository and run these. None of them touch the network, and none need a CoinMarketCap credential. Each of the first three finished in well under a second on every machine this has been run on; the gate's own range is stated with it below, because that one is measured and pinned by a test rather than quoted from one run.

git clone https://github.com/dyplux/bell.git && cd bell
make base-rate
Under a second. Prints the whole-catalogue measurement: 791 references, 547 with a single representation and nothing to compare, 244 where a comparison could be attempted, 157 refused, 87 published. Refusal rate 64.3%, Wilson 95% interval 58.2 to 70.1%. 
python3 bell/verify_rule_boundaries.py
Under a second. An executable specification of the decision boundaries: 1.99x stays below the dispersion rule, 2x is an inclusive warning, 9.99x stays a warning, 10x is an inclusive stop, and missing or zero prices do not fabricate a ratio. All 8 checks pass. 
make demo
Under a second. Recomputes the 50 highest-priority flagged references from the dated replay receipt and prints 35 refused, 10 comparable, 5 held for investigation, with the reasoning for each. Those 50 are a priority-sorted slice, not a sample: the references carrying no signal are not in the list, so the three counts are not a rate. The rate over the whole catalogue, with its denominator, is the command above. 
make check-offline
The gate runs between 2.8 and 12.0 seconds across the machines it has been measured on, cold runs included. This band has been widened twice rather than quoted tight: a first run on a cold machine was measured at 10.3 seconds, and the stated figure has to cover the run a reader gets, not the best one.

What that gate proves, and what it does not

The suite
378 tests, 280 Python and 98 JavaScript. One Python test needs a network and a CoinMarketCap key; without them it is skipped and the runner says so.
The re-derivation
The published receipt is recomputed from the 16.5 MB of shipped inputs. The inputs are verified byte for byte and the scan is recomputed from them, so a single changed count fails here.
The submission gate
Runs a submission gate over 160 tracked files, inspecting every one for anything private or unreleasable.
One observation is UNVERIFIED
Of the 14 dated observations, 2 ship their full payload. One of those two has its state distribution compared against the receipt. The other does not: it was written under the rule set that ran before this one, and no code here can re-derive it. Its totals, signals and source digests are checked; the split between do_not_compare, investigate and no_flags is not, and the verifier prints UNVERIFIED rather than leaving that to be inferred.
The chain
every one of the 14 is chained to the one before it, so editing a single record invalidates every digest after it.
The chain is not a signature
It defeats a quiet single-record edit. It does not defeat anyone who rewrites the whole file, the chain and the committed anchor together, because all three live in this repository. There is no external attestation and no date attestation, and the module says so in its own docstring.
Rule changes are not market changes
Where a receipt predates the current rule set, the recomputed state counts are reported beside the recorded ones rather than compared, because they answer to different rules.

Two searches that show both halves

On the product page, search these two. They are the affirmative and the refusal, and both hold on the dated replay receipt in this repository as well as on the live one, so a local clone shows what this page describes.

  1. Coinbase Global, Inc. returns COMPARABLE: the representations share an identity, a unit and a market state, so Bell names the spread in basis points, the cheapest route, and whether that route is also the deepest. This is the output the product exists to produce.
  2. Silver returns DO NOT SHORTLIST: its representations are quoted at a ratio above the inclusive 10x block floor, which is a unit or claim mismatch rather than a bargain. Bell names the rule that fired, keeps the example amount uncommitted, and states the next check rather than ranking the wrappers.

What it refuses to do

A monitor that refuses is only worth reading if you know how often, so the rate is measured over the whole catalogue rather than argued from examples. The refusals are also split rather than totalled: 120 of the 158 reasons are a second price CoinMarketCap never published, a coverage fact about the source, and 38 are rows that contradict each other. Reported as one number it would read as an indictment of the market when most of it is an incomplete catalogue.

The same discipline applies to Bell's own history. Where two published receipts were produced under different rule sets, the page refuses to print a delta between them and says so, because subtracting them would state a market change nobody observed.

THE BOUNDARY

A clean result is descriptive only. Bell does not prove backing, redemption, custody, legal eligibility, solvency or executable liquidity, and it is not a safety score or an investment recommendation. Where the Startup plan does not return a surface, such as RWA market pairs, that is stated rather than filled in.

Which CMC surfaces it uses

All 7 of the dedicated RWA endpoints are used by this repository, and 17 distinct CoinMarketCap endpoints in total. The published population scan calls five of the seven: map, assets list, quotes latest, info and issuers list, joined on stable identifiers rather than on tickers, because a ticker is reused across representations. cryptocurrency/info resolves chain and contract identity, which the RWA family does not carry.

An exported case receipt names, for each surface, the endpoint, the payload digest, how many requests were made, how many returned, the HTTP status codes with their counts and the request window. Those come from the replay package's collection manifest, so the artefact states which observation they describe rather than implying they measured whatever scan is loaded. Credentials and transport headers are recorded nowhere, which is the one thing that cannot be published without breaking the credential-free claim.

Be precise about what that record is worth, because a reviewer was right to press on it. The payload digest is recomputable: the payload ships, so anyone can hash it and check. The request counts, status codes and per-response digests are self-declared. The individual HTTP responses are not shipped, so nothing in this repository can recompute them, and a verifier that only rejects obvious placeholders is not verifying them. They are recorded so a reader knows what the collection looked like, not offered as proof that it did. Shipping the raw responses would make them checkable and would also mean publishing what the credential returned, which is the trade this project has not made.

The other two are deliberately outside that loop rather than missing. market-pairs/list is not returned on the Startup plan, and the receipt says so in words instead of filling the field with a zero. issuers, the single-issuer endpoint, is used by the per-reference audit path in bell/rwa_audit.py, which also reaches the DEX family. Historical OHLCV lives in bell/bell.py and key/info in bell/live_contract_probe.py. Grep the repository and count: the list above is what you will find.

The join is where the work is. Reconciling the 7,811 rows the map returns against the 7,942 the asset list returns exposes 131 rows with no stable reference ID, which is the kind of surface disagreement a price-first tool never sees because it drops those rows before it starts.
