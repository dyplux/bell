# Before the shortlist: check whether the wrapper can be compared

Tokenised real-world assets can share a reference, a ticker or a familiar name while representing different instruments, units or markets. A price table makes them look easy to compare. It does not, by itself, show whether that comparison is well formed.

Bell is an evidence gate for the step before an RWA shortlist. It reads CoinMarketCap's RWA reference map, token representations and quote fields, joins records by stable identifiers, and routes each reference through explicit checks. The result is a research handoff: what the observed fields support, what they do not support, and what a researcher should verify next.

## A reference is a starting point

CoinMarketCap provides discovery surfaces for tokenised real-world assets. Bell uses the RWA map and asset list to identify references and representation rows, then reads quote data and available token and issuer metadata. It joins RWA references, token rows and issuer fields by their identifiers; a matching ticker alone is not a join key.

The public product separates the live receipt from the dated evidence archive. The receipt records its observation and publication times, source fingerprints, scan counts and the state of each reference. A reviewer can inspect the full population or start with an individual reference in the [Bell workspace](https://bell.dyplux.com/workspace.html).

## The route is the useful output

Bell has several possible routes. `DO NOT SHORTLIST` means a coded stop rule or a critical evidence gap blocks the comparison. `INVESTIGATE` means identity or market fields need follow-up. `FACTS OPEN` is descriptive and permits a filtered comparison of reported quotes where the comparison rows pass Bell's price and volume filters. A single representation has no cross-wrapper comparison to make.

These labels describe data handling. They do not establish that a token is safe or unsafe. In particular, a filtered price comparison does not establish equivalent units or rights, backing, redemption, custody, solvency, eligibility, market depth or executable size. Reported 24-hour volume is not liquidity available to trade.

When a comparison is published, Bell shows which rows entered it, the observed quote spread, and which route has the highest reported 24-hour volume. When the evidence is incomplete or trips a stop rule, the product keeps the wrappers separate and names the next diligence task. Missing values remain missing rather than being silently converted to zero.

## One dated scan, not a leaderboard

The latest receipt observed on **28 September 2026 at 22:16 UTC** scanned **793 tokenised references and 1,452 representation rows**. Bell routed **34 references to `DO NOT SHORTLIST`, 102 to `INVESTIGATE`, and 657 to `FACTS OPEN`** under that receipt's rules.

Those counts describe that observation and its rule set. They are not a ranking of issuers, a quality score, or a claim about the entire tokenised-asset market. The receipt is refreshed independently; the website labels a later or older observation with its publication state and time. The dated package is available from the site's evidence section so the published observation can be checked separately from a future live receipt.

## From screen to diligence

For a researcher, the practical sequence is short:

1. Search the underlying stock, ETF or commodity.
2. Read the route and the token and issuer rows that produced it.
3. Compare only filtered quote rows, and preserve the distinction between quote spread and executable price.
4. Carry unresolved unit, issuer, redemption, venue and settlement questions into the next diligence step.

Bell does not replace issuer documentation, legal review, reserve verification or venue analysis. It makes the limits of the data visible before a shortlist makes a clean table look like a clean answer.
